## 1. Campaign overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Campaign name** | **"Take the Bet"** — the book's own frame: it doesn't arrive with an answer, it arrives with a bet, and it invites readers to test it. |
| **One-line summary** | Turn L&D leaders who are already uneasy about AI into pre-order buyers, team-pack buyers and test-runners of the book's experiment. |
| **Primary objective** | ⚠ **1,000 copies sold through the site (pre-order + first 60 days)**, measured in Shopify. |
| **Secondary objectives** | 2,500 email subscribers · 25 team/bulk orders (10+ copies) · 10 podcast or newsletter appearances · 50 reader reviews in the first 90 days. |

## 2. Target audience

**Primary: the L&D builder.** Instructional designers, learning experience designers, L&D managers and directors at companies with 100+ employees. They build the courses the book questions. They're curious, a bit anxious about AI and their jobs, and short on proof.
> *"An L&D manager at a mid-size company who is under pressure to 'do something with AI' and wants to know whether it helps people learn or only makes them faster, and what to build next. They find ideas on LinkedIn, in L&D podcasts and newsletters, and at ATD/DevLearn, and they care about evidence they can bring to their boss."*

**Secondary: the budget owner.** CLOs, CHROs, HR and talent leaders, and the managers paying for training ($102.8B in U.S. spend in 2025). They care about time to readiness and whether training changes on-the-job behavior (Kirkpatrick Levels 3–4).

**Tertiary: the AI-curious operator.** Ops leaders, enablement and customer-support leads who already use AI assistants and want people to learn rather than only lean on the tool.

| Pain point | What the book offers |
|---|---|
| "AI is going to make my job obsolete." | "I'm not saying L&D is gone. I'm saying it's drastically changed." A clear picture of *A Different Job* (ch. 33). |
| "Our courses don't change behavior." | Training without transfer (ch. 3), the scoreboard (ch. 4) and Levels 3 & 4 (ch. 30). |
| "Everyone's selling AI hype." | Both sides of the research: +15% problems solved/hour with AI, −17% when it's taken away. "Anyone who shows you only one of them is selling something." |
| "I need something I can actually run." | Appendix A: *The Kit*, a two-group test a team can run itself, and Appendix C: *Questions for Your Team*. |

**Where they spend time:** LinkedIn (main channel) · L&D podcasts and newsletters · ATD, DevLearn, Learning Technologies, Brandon Hall HCM Excellence · L&D Slack and Discord communities · Training magazine and Chief Learning Officer.

**Buying stage:** most are *problem-aware* (courses aren't working, AI is coming) but not *solution-aware*. The campaign moves them from awareness ("the course is over?") to consideration (free chapter / Questions for Your Team) to decision (pre-order, team pack).

## 3. Key messages

**Core message:** *For everyday corporate learning, the course is no longer needed to build capability. The learning still is. The teaching moves into the work.*

| Supporting message | Proof point |
|---|---|
| **1. Learn the job by doing the job.** Real task, verified knowledge, and an AI coach that teaches and then backs off, like training wheels. | Intro "Training Wheels"; Part Two, *The Science of Doing* (scaffolding, Two Sigma, teach-back). |
| **2. This is a bet, not a sales pitch.** It names what would prove it wrong. | The four-group A/B test; "If the people who took no course can't do the job without the AI, I lose." Part Four, *The Case Against Me*. |
| **3. Help that is built to leave.** Someone who can only do the job while the AI runs hasn't learned it. | The −17% math-tutor study; ch. 23 *When the Power Goes Out*. |
| **4. Written by a builder, not a pundit.** 20+ years building training in health care, consulting and higher ed. | Two 2026 Brandon Hall silvers (Leadership Redefined; Futures Learning Hub); Training magazine 2017 (Story Sync). |

**Tone by channel:** LinkedIn: provocative, first-person, short ("I built courses for 20 years. Here's my bet that we're done."). Email: warm and candid, behind the scenes. Podcasts and press: evidence-led, both sides. Ads: one bold claim plus the cover.

**Messages to avoid:** "AI replaces trainers," "no more training," and anything about high-stakes or clinical learning. The book explicitly carves those out.

## 8. Budget allocation

No budget yet, so the shares in §4 are a starter split: about 65% paid distribution, 25% production (ARCs, design, trailer) and 10% contingency. Move money monthly toward whichever of lead ads or retargeting has the lower cost per sale.

## 9. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Book isn't final** (draft 32); copy or chapters change | The site pulls book copy from one place. Only title, premise and audience are locked, which is what the campaign leans on. |
| **"Anti-trainer" misread** angers the core audience | Lead with "It's drastically changed, not gone" and the high-stakes carve-out; feature *A Different Job*. |
| **No social proof at launch** | ARCs in week 2; until blurbs arrive, use the author's awards and the book's own evidence. Never fake praise. |
| **Paid ads underperform** for a niche B2B book | Gate spend on lead-ad CPL < $6 in week 4; otherwise move budget into podcast sponsorships. |
| **Vanderbilt affiliation** | Follow the book's disclaimer: personal views only, no university branding or endorsement on the site. |
